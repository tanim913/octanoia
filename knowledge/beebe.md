## PART A. The model

### A.1 Function versus attitude (Ch. 1, 3, 7, 10, 13, 14)

- **Function** means one of four kinds of consciousness: sensation, thinking, feeling, intuition. **Attitude** means "the habitual way the attention is directed—whether extraverted or introverted" (Ch. 10, note 3). Putting the two together gives eight "function-attitudes." Beebe credits Dick Thompson (1996) for the term.
- **For Jung the attitude came first**: "for Jung the attitude type was the primary thing, and the function type a kind of sub-something that expressed that attitude in a particular way" (Ch. 7).
- **Jung's short definitions** (Ch. 10): "Sensation tells us that a thing is. Thinking tells us what the thing is, feeling tells us what it is worth to us." Intuition adds time: "Things have a past and they have a future... you get what the Americans call a hunch."
- **Rational versus irrational.** Thinking and feeling are rational, meaning evaluative. Sensation and intuition are irrational, meaning they perceive what is given. Marshall's terms are "functions of the given" versus "functions of option" (Ch. 10). "We do not rationally choose what we manage to see, hear, smell, taste."
- **Feeling is not emotion.** "Feeling, I tell my audiences, is the function that sorts out feelings" (Ch. 2). Feeling is "neither affect... nor the result of more unconscious emotion-based processes" (Ch. 10). This matters for item writing: an emotional item is not a feeling-function item.
- **Extraversion merges with the object. Introversion checks the object against an inner archetype.** "Extraversion moves to create a shared experience, by reaching out to 'merge' in some way with the other person..., whereas introversion steps back from the experience to see if it 'matches' an archetype within that carries an a priori understanding of what an experience like this is supposed to consist of" (Ch. 3, "Types of the shadow").
- **What the introverted functions match against.** The inner "idea" an introverted function checks against "can be understood as a profound thought, a value, a metaphorical image, or a model of reality, depending upon whether the introverted function is thinking, feeling, intuition or sensation" (Ch. 10, Definitions).
- **Disappointment is normal for introverted functions.** The object "often doesn't live up to the archetype," which explains "the frequent disappointment that introverted functions register... a disappointment that must not be confused with a condition to be treated... It's their normal way of reacting" (Ch. 13, Extraversion and introversion).
- **Introverted versus extraverted in Ch. 1.** Introverted processes are "internal methods by which they have already learned to deal with what they encounter." Extraverted processes are "methods by which they enact a genuine encounter with the Other."
- **The hardest pair to understand is the same function in the opposite attitude.** Von Franz, quoted in Ch. 3: "the hardest thing to understand is not your opposite type... but the same functional type with the other attitude!" This is the basis of Beebe's shadow model.
- **Time orientation** (Mann, Siegler & Osmond, cited in Ch. 10): sensation looks at the present, feeling at the past, intuition at the future, and thinking runs continuously across all three.

### A.2 The eight archetypal positions

Beebe's two observations (Ch. 8): (1) "we each actually make use of all the function-attitudes," and (2) they "are not expressed in the same way by every individual," because both strength and role vary.

| # | Position (Jung's term) | Archetype | Axis | Ego-relation | How the function feels and behaves here (Beebe) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Superior / dominant | **Hero** (man) / **Heroine** (woman) | Spine (top) | Ego-syntonic | "Most ready to claim ownership of... a sense of competence and potential mastery... welcomes facing challenges, takes pleasure in recalling its past successful exploits, revels in its unflagging reliability" (Ch. 8). It asserts the self rather than caring for others (Ch. 13). A "superiority complex" can form around it. It differentiates in childhood, and more so when the family values it. |
| 2 | Auxiliary | **Good Father / Good Mother** | Arms (right arm) | Ego-syntonic | "Parental; it takes the lead in fostering the development of other people, and it often serves as their role model." It is "not so good at taking care of the third function in oneself, but... operates like a good parent to everyone else" (Ch. 9). Its aim is "taking care of another" (Ch. 13). |
| 3 | Tertiary | **Puer aeternus / Puella aeterna** (eternal child) | Arms (left hand) | Ego-syntonic, but archaic and "god-like entitled" | "Sometimes original and creative, but always a bit unstable and at times even weaker in its reliability than the inferior function... acutely aware of its need for the stabilizing influence of another person" (Ch. 9). It cycles between inflation and deflation, which Beebe calls the "third-function crisis." It is the seat of "a traumatic neurosis that I have come to feel we probably all carry." |
| 4 | Inferior | **Anima** (man) / **Animus** (woman) | Spine (bottom) | Ego-syntonic, though carrying unconscious values | "A perpetual source of shame or embarrassment"; forced use "can be an agony" and draws "a yelp of complaint" (Ch. 8). It is also "a place of great idealism... The higher cause or mission that seizes our energy." It is the bridge to the unconscious and the Self, and the seat of integrity. It is "least under the control of our good intentions, slowest to take training" (Ch. 3). It develops mostly in the second half of life. |
| 5 | Shadow of 1 | **Opposing personality** | Shadow spine | Ego-dystonic | "Paranoid, avoidant, passive-aggressive and seductive" (Ch. 8); also histrionic (Ch. 4). "A primary resource of defense, a part of us that tends to lurch forward first when we feel our heroic superior function and its most cherished values to be under attack." It "seems to fall in the blind spot of the superior function" and is "very easy to project onto another person, especially a person of the opposite sex" (Ch. 3). It is "more like a symptom than like a dashing enemy on a black horse." |
| 6 | Shadow of 2 | **Senex** (critical old man) / **Witch** (critical mother) | Shadow arms | Ego-dystonic | "It discourages and disables them; it freezes them in their tracks and makes them doubt the prudence of everything they are doing" (Ch. 9). It "pulls rank," is "dogmatic and mean," a "withering authority." It is "sometimes a necessary one" for setting limits on what is really destructive. |
| 7 | Shadow of 3 | **Trickster** | Shadow arms | Ego-dystonic | It puts people in double binds, reverses expectations, and toys with rules. "So long as it remains unconscious... one is vulnerable to being taken advantage of." "It is an enormous step in type development when we are able to make the trickster conscious and put the person who is trying to take advantage of us in a double bind" (Ch. 9). |
| 8 | Shadow of 4 | **Demonic / Daimonic personality** | Shadow spine | Ego-dystonic | "Operates in the shadow to undermine others and ourselves" (Ch. 3). It is "usually the locus of our most unyielding and unconscious flaws of character. When we act beastly, it is often through this archetype" (Ch. 8). It is "both devil and angel": it can bring "insights that are of the highest value." It is the Beast that "stays a beast, but one that to some extent can be tamed through the anima's solicitous and energetic care." |

**Structural rules** (Ch. 3 list; Ch. 9 note 3; Ch. 10):
1. If position 1 is rational, position 2 is irrational, and vice versa.
2. Position 2 takes the opposite attitude from position 1 (following Myers).
3. Position 3 is the opposite function to position 2, with the opposite attitude to position 2, which is the same attitude as position 1.
4. Position 4 is the opposite function to position 1, with the opposite attitude.
5. Positions 5 to 8 repeat functions 1 to 4 with the attitude flipped. "By 'shadow'... I mean having the same function but the opposite attitude" (Ch. 8).
6. The **spine** (1–4) is "the axis of our relation to self" and is about identity and integrity. The **arms** (2–3) are "the axis of our relations to others" and are about "ways of caring and being cared for." One axis is always rational and the other irrational, so the arms can "calm us down" when the spine is irrational, or "buoy us beyond the rigidity of the spine when it is rational" (Ch. 9).

**Standard dialogue pairs** (Ch. 14): Hero–Anima; Good Father–Puer; Senex–Trickster; Hero versus Opposing; Anima versus Demonic; Puer versus Trickster; Good Mother versus Witch; Opposing versus Demonic. Gender in the shadow roles is "not invariable"; for example, senex versus trickster can appear in a woman.

**The numbers are not a strength ranking.** "I no longer view the type profile of an individual as expressing a hierarchy of differentiation... It is as if they form an interacting cast of characters" (Ch. 7). The numbers "are meant to be read as qualitative rather than quantitative... like avenues in New York City" (Ch. 9). "The strength, and the kind of strength, a function of consciousness displays is a consequence of the archetypal role associated with it, and archetypes are differently developed in different people" (Ch. 9).

## PART B. The eight function-attitudes

Beebe's keywords (Ch. 1) come in three stages: **persona level → ego "heart" → Self-level goal.** "The first... what the process looks like on the surface... The second, central, word captures the heart of the process... The third word embodies what the process becomes at its most evolved level."

| | Persona | Heart | Goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Se | engaging | **experiencing** | enjoying |
| Si | implementing | **verifying** | accounting |
| Ne | entertaining | **envisioning** | enabling |
| Ni | imagining | **knowing** | divining |
| Te | regulating | **planning** | enforcing |
| Ti | naming | **defining** | understanding |
| Fe | validating | **affirming** | relating |
| Fi | judging | **appraising** | establishing the value |

Beebe's advice is to anchor recognition on the central word.

[derived] **Design idea:** each function can carry three tiers of items: persona-level behavior, core process, and mature goal. Endorsing all three tiers suggests a developed function. Endorsing only the persona tier may mean learned behavior.

### 1. Ne: extraverted intuition (envisioning)

**Core definition.** Ne reads the possibilities live in a person, object or situation in front of you. It picks up what is not yet there, often "what was going on in other people's minds," and sees potential "that others might never have imagined." It starts as entertaining (amusing others and entertaining ideas), commits through envisioning, and matures into enabling other people to see and use those possibilities (Ch. 1, Ch. 3). Beebe's metaphor is a traffic signal that only the Ne person can see: green means develop it now, red means stop, yellow means proceed with caution (Ch. 10).

**Behavioral markers [derived]:**
1. Mid-conversation, you jump to "what if we..." before the other person has finished describing the situation as it is.
2. A new project, book, film or idea pulls you in even when you're already tired and "it's time to go home." You cross your own comfort limits chasing the next thing.
3. You sense where someone is "staking their future" and talk to that rather than to their current complaint.
4. You start writing or talking in the middle of things with "nervy urgency," without setting up the context.
5. You can find "some possibility for coping with any problem," and scarcity or dead ends feel unreal to you until they hit.
6. Your timing of when to push, pause or drop something can look arbitrary to others because they don't see your signal.
7. You eat, walk or move through a space without noticing it, absorbed in plans (the engineer at Sharp's dinner party, Ch. 13).
8. Your ideas often get carried out by other people. You launch more than you finish.
9. You enjoy amusing a group by entertaining ideas aloud.

**What it is NOT / common confusions:**
- It is not extraverted feeling. People mistook Jo Wheelwright's Ne combined with his introverted feeling auxiliary for Fe, "conflating the extraversion of his superior function... with the availability or readiness of his auxiliary" (Ch. 13).
- It is not plain intrusiveness or narcissism, though "some thought Jo's extraverted intuition was simply intrusiveness, or narcissism" (Ch. 13). Ne "can feel intrusive, but it is also entertaining and astonishing" (Ch. 13).
- It is not Ni. Ne works from possibilities in what is shared outside. Ni looks at gestalts in the unconscious (Ch. 3).
- Its weak spot: "failure to heed sensation cues can undermine its claim to have 'seen' anything at all" (Ch. 10). Being optimistic is not the same as perceiving accurately.

**Ne in shadow and stress positions:**
- **Witch/Senex Ne** (ENFJ, ESFJ). The Wicked Witch of the West: "constantly threatening Dorothy with what she is going to do to her... promise of bad things to come... with the anticipatory delight of her evil cackle." The winged monkeys are "an intuition used to terrify others with its unexpected ability to get to them." The Witch is "always inserting herself into Dorothy's private spaces and thoughts," eliciting and then mocking her fears in the crystal ball (Ch. 5). [derived] In daily life: forecasting disaster to stop someone's plan, or catastrophizing aloud at a person.
- **Opposing Ne** (INTJ, INFJ). Sally in *Husbands and Wives* "draws unexpected conclusions through an irrational process of rapidly shifting standpoints... continually leaps ahead of her body and her feeling... so intensely oppositional to what everyone around her is expecting as to become a figure of fun" (Ch. 14). [derived] Contrarian "but what about..." used to block or avoid commitment, not to explore.
- **Trickster Ne** (ISTP, ISFP). [derived] Throwing out alternative possibilities to double-bind or wriggle out of obligations.
- **Demonic Ne** (Se-dominant types: ESTP, ESFP). Ne "tends to take on a perversely destabilizing role, leading him to embrace the wrong possibilities and reject the right ones, except when, very occasionally, it is the source of a moment of unexpected good sense" (Ch. 4).
- **Inferior Ne** (Si-dominant types). It "may often fail to deliver the larger picture to him of where things overall are going" (Ch. 4). Its idealism shows as concern for "the quality of the future, for instance by acting now to reduce global warming" (Ch. 8).
- **Shadowy Ne in Jung's Red Book.** The tramp "looks outside himself for images that will help him to make sense of his soul's turmoil, a strategy Jung finds lazy" (Ch. 11).

**Stories and metaphors:**
- **Lyndon Johnson** appeared in Beebe's dream as his hero Ne: "high-handed, crafty... a bit out of touch with the actual readiness of those around me for the changes that I wanted to introduce in their lives" (Ch. 7).
- **The gambling husband** of the Chinese laundress: someone who "pursues possibilities and takes his energy into the world, leaving his introverted wife at home alone" (Ch. 3). Beebe first read this as his Ne and later re-read it as his demonic Se.
- **Mr. Hulot's Holiday** (Tati): a "likeable, bumbling, extraverted intuitive" lights fireworks. Ne combined with Se is the "aesthetic attitude," "Beauty as an explosion of energy, perfectly contained" (Ch. 6).
- **Bagdad Café**: Brenda is an Ne puella (Ch. 11).
- **Beebe's career**: he "entertained" type theory in 1968, "envisioned" a model in 1973, and later found himself "enabling audiences" (Ch. 1).
- **Jo Wheelwright** (ENFP by Beebe's reading).

**Key quotes:**
- "My extraverted intuition (Ne) often begins its action as a mental process, with a stage of 'entertaining' that applies in two senses of the word: (1) interesting and amusing myself and others; and (2) beginning to consider or 'entertain' possibilities" (Ch. 1).
- "Extraverted intuition can become so engaged with the possibilities of its objects that, for the person strongly identified with this function, 'it is as though his whole life vanished in the new situation'" (Ch. 10, Definitions, quoting Jung ¶613).
- "Extraverted intuition can feel intrusive, but it is also entertaining and astonishing in the way it can pick up on fresh possibilities for developing the objectives of the therapy in the world" (Ch. 13, Extraversion and introversion).

### 2. Ni: introverted intuition (knowing)

**Core definition.** Ni perceives the archetypal image behind a situation directly, "as if 'seeing' it." It attends to "the background processes of consciousness," and for this function "unconscious images acquire the dignity of things." It sees "the big picture in the unconscious, where the gestalts that moved nations, religions, and epochs lay, even in the midst of apparently 'individual' experience." It moves from imagining to knowing to divining. Divining has two senses: "seeing in what direction the future is bending" and "descrying the divine purpose hidden in the developing situation" (Ch. 1 note 2; Ch. 3; Ch. 10). It matches experience to "an image of an archetype, something like a visual metaphor" (Ch. 13).

**Behavioral markers [derived]:**
1. You trust your own perception of what is "real, fundamental, and of lasting importance over what others may see and think" (Ch. 6).
2. An image or metaphor arrives whole, and you then watch "how the picture changes, unfolds, and finally fades" (Ch. 12, quoting Jung).
3. Insight comes in sudden epiphanies of the "this means me, my life" kind, which you defend strongly (Jung's house dream, Ch. 13).
4. You hold back what you foresee "until the time seems right to share it" (Glinda, Ch. 5).
5. When you explain something, you reach for mythic, symbolic or historical patterns, and listeners sometimes feel lost "in a maze of... amplifications" (Ch. 10).
6. You make global, categorical judgments about where a situation or a life is heading.
7. Your wit lands on the hidden meaning of a situation rather than its surface (Gabe, Ch. 14).
8. You get uneasy hunches about places or moments ("a peculiar feeling... at the next corner," Jung's driving example, Ch. 3).

**What it is NOT / common confusions:**
- Clinicians confuse it with Fi and Si: "introverted feeling, introverted sensation, and introverted intuition are easily confused with each other" (Ch. 13). "Confusing introverted feeling with introverted intuition" is common (Ch. 10).
- The difference from Fi: "Introverted feeling, by contrast, can only feel the archetypal image of a situation. It cannot see it" (Ch. 10).
- It is not mysticism or crankiness, though Jung calls the type "the mystical dreamer and seer on the one hand, the artist and the crank on the other" (Ch. 12).
- It is the most culturally devalued function: "Of all eight kinds of consciousness, introverted intuition is the one that is most consistently devalued in contemporary Western culture" (Ch. 12). Howard Gardner left it out of his multiple intelligences.

**Ni in shadow and stress positions:**
- **Opposing Ni** (ENTP, ENFP; Beebe's own). He became "detached and avoidant in a schizoid way" and would "'tune out' in the face of affects I didn't know how to deal with... my patients experienced me at such moments as leaving them." Friends said he got more "John-ish" (Ch. 3). Opposing Ni is "avoidant, passive–aggressive, paranoid and seductive" (Ch. 7). He projected it onto Ni women who wouldn't "come clean" with their intuitions. When asked to write about archetypes, which is Ni territory, he used "somewhat negative, oppositional examples" (Ch. 8).
- **Trickster Ni** (ESFJ, ESTJ). A parent can "imply damage to the archetypal order of things when an adolescent child threatens to make a choice that the parent does not approve of," for example telling a daughter who might marry outside the family pattern that she would be "destroying the very sacredness of family." Yet the parent "has never previously shown an objection to anything on such a spiritual basis" (Ch. 9).
- **Witch/Senex Ni** (ENTJ, INTP). [derived] Ominous, sweeping pronouncements used to pull rank.
- **Demonic Ni** (ISTJ, ISFJ). A Si-dominant man hears an inner voice: "You're nothing. You're just nothing!" These are "global, archetypal assessments, to which there was no right of appeal." Once he confronted it, it turned daimonic: he "took a look at the big picture of his career" and changed careers (Ch. 4).
- **Inferior Ni** (Se-dominant types). [derived from the general rule] Vague foreboding, and idealism about meaning.

**Stories and metaphors:**
- **Glinda the Good**: "reassuringly self-contained, bubble-enclosed, introverted intuition used to solace and focus Dorothy without intruding on the girl's autonomy." The Yellow Brick Road is an image of intuition, since yellow is intuition's color (Ch. 5).
- **Jung's house dream** on the trip to America: "It was not Freud's dream, it was mine." Beebe calls it a burst of heroic Ni, with "narcissism in this as well as a certain heroic combativeness" (Ch. 13).
- **The Siegfried dream**: Jung killed his "false" Te heroism so that his true Ni could lead. Using thinking as his superior function had been "a falsification of type" (Ch. 11).
- **Elijah**, emblem of Ni, with blind Salome (Se) as his daughter (Ch. 12).
- **Gabe** (Woody Allen) in *Husbands and Wives* (Ch. 14). **Jasmin**, the Ni trickster in *Bagdad Café* (Ch. 11).
- "Had this type not existed there would have been no prophets in Israel" (Jung, quoted in Ch. 12).

**Key quotes:**
- "Introverted intuition... looked at the big picture in the unconscious, where the gestalts that moved nations, religions, and epochs lay, even in the midst of apparently 'individual' experience" (Ch. 3, Type development).
- "The introverted intuitive function becomes directly aware of the archetype as an image, as if 'seeing' it: introverted intuition is therefore the function responsible for visionary experience, which often seems 'mystical' to others" (Ch. 10, Definitions).
- "Introverted intuition seeks to match up the experience with an image of an archetype, something like a visual metaphor" (Ch. 13).

### 3. Se: extraverted sensation (experiencing)

**Core definition.** Se is a direct, embodied encounter with physical reality: "compelling, often shared, experiences of the textures, smells, sights, sounds, and tastes of the world—a direct relationship with reality" (Ch. 3). It goes from engaging to experiencing to enjoying. Something becomes real to Se only after it has been touched and enjoyed. Se is "the difference between talking about it and getting to know it intimately" (Ch. 1). It seeks "an accumulation of actual experiences of concrete objects" (Ch. 10, quoting Jung).

**Behavioral markers [derived]:**
1. You only feel you understand something once you've handled it, tried it, or been there.
2. You lose patience with abstract explanations, "as if what is already there is sufficient without much interpretation" (Ch. 13).
3. You're fully absorbed in what is happening right now (a game, a meal, a room), sometimes so absorbed that you miss a side development coming (the basketball example, Ch. 10).
4. Being able to enjoy something physically, like food, scenery or texture, is a big part of how you value it.
5. You take care of people concretely: cooking for them, fixing things, setting up the tent (Ch. 9).
6. You respond to a problem by acting physically and immediately rather than reflecting.
7. Inner states that can't be tied to something tangible seem suspect to you, and you "breathe again" once back in tangible reality (Ch. 12, quoting Jung).
8. You notice and relish sensory detail in your surroundings: smell, light, sound ("I enjoy elder-blossoms, the smell of hay, and the silence," Ch. 14).

**What it is NOT / common confusions:**
- It is not hedonism or thrill-seeking. "Extraverted sensation on its own, however, might lead merely to thrill-seeking or hedonism, were it not combined with something else" (Ch. 6). Enjoying is its mature goal, not an indulgence: it "has a hint of the spiritual to it" (Ch. 1).
- It is not Si. Detloff "wondered why they were even both called 'sensation'" (Ch. 3). Se reaches out to the shared world; Si checks inner bodily comfort and order against an inner standard.
- It is not shallow: "Patients... for whom that function-attitude is dominant often cannot follow my rather abstract introverted thinking. It's just too disembodied to be tangible to them" (Ch. 1).

**Se in shadow and stress positions:**
- **Witch/Senex Se** (ESTJ, ESFJ). Someone with Se in the witch position "might be tempted to express displeasure by banging loudly on the wall of an apartment when the neighbor's party... is too loud, or by slapping an impudent child" (Ch. 9).
- **Demonic Se** (ENTP, ENFP; Beebe's own). "My body language is often the opposite of what I mean to convey... when trying to find my way along an unfamiliar route, the opposite of where I think I should be going is almost always the correct way." More seriously, he misjudges how ready a patient is to hear something, which can "shock the client." Occasionally this "enliven[s] a therapy that has become too polite" (Ch. 3). Mr. Hulot's demonic Se sets off chaos with the fireworks (Ch. 6).
- **Puer Se** (ENFJ, ENTJ). The Cowardly Lion: "physical size, his bullying swagger, and his need to impose his body on others ('Put 'em up, put 'em up!')," swinging between "boastful omnipotence ('I'll be King of the Forest') and cowering terror" (Ch. 5).
- **Opposing Se** (ISTJ, ISFJ). [derived] Stubborn, physical, passive-aggressive refusal, such as digging in your heels.
- **Inferior Se** (Ni-dominant types). Idealism: "The person who cares most passionately about the quality and safety of food may have superior introverted intuition and thus inferior extraverted sensation" (Ch. 8). Jung's anima Salome is blind, "petty, hysterical, and concrete" (Ch. 12).

**Stories and metaphors:**
- **Salome** in Jung's Red Book as his Se soul (Ch. 11–12).
- **Rain**, the student in *Husbands and Wives*, "brings a fresh sensuality to her every screen moment" (Ch. 14).
- **The industrialist** at Sharp's dinner party: "well dressed but loud, and a greedy though appreciative eater" (Ch. 13).
- **The Chicago Art Institute**: Beebe sustained his morale in medical school by connecting to Se "through beauty in a totally satisfying way" (Ch. 6).
- **Fi combined with Se** gives "a style of emotional realism that prefers action to reflection and can produce an explosive rather than thought-out reaction when confronted with a deeply felt injustice" (Ch. 15, on *Do the Right Thing*).

**Key quotes:**
- "Extraverted sensation in its stages of engaging, experiencing, and enjoying the reality of something is the difference between talking about it and getting to know it intimately. It involves touching and being touched by every part of it—a very different thing from a simply mental representation" (Ch. 1).
- "Only if they have enjoyed something sufficiently, by bringing it near to themselves in an embodied way, and have found it, when truly touched, to be a source of concrete satisfaction, does it seem sufficiently real to them" (Ch. 1).
- "This is a function perfect for watching a basketball game, but it may not notice that someone is about to say or do something unexpected" (Ch. 10).

### 4. Si: introverted sensation (verifying)

**Core definition.** Si checks whether experience matches "an inner sense of what has already been established through long human experience as 'real'" (Ch. 13). It "concerns itself primarily with finding order, organizing experience, and monitoring the comfort of the body on the inside" (Ch. 3). It goes from implementing to verifying to accounting. It reacts to the inner bodily effect a stimulus produces more than to the stimulus itself (Ch. 10), and it attends to small details to confirm that a process is running correctly (Ch. 4).

**Behavioral markers [derived]:**
1. At a dinner, how full you feel, how spicy the food is, or how loud the table is decides your enjoyment more than who is there (Ch. 10).
2. You track whether each step of a job is being done "correctly," moment by moment, and you spot deviations from the right procedure (Ch. 4).
3. You keep work "flowing within budget." You like accounts, records and reconciliations (Ch. 4).
4. You notice fine differences between similar things: two paintings, two versions, two batches (the art history professor at Sharp's dinner party, Ch. 13).
5. You protect your body from being overstimulated, overheated, tired, hungry or wrongly fed (Ch. 3).
6. You get "a clear picture very quickly and then withdraw so as not to be overwhelmed" (Ch. 12, quoting Jung).
7. You help others by teaching "the series of simple steps," such as how to knot a tie (Ch. 9).
8. You trust your own sensory evidence against what other people argue (Toto vouching for Dorothy, Ch. 5).
9. You can "soldier on" through discomfort by focusing on the task, which works as a stoic defense (Ch. 4).

**What it is NOT / common confusions:**
- It is not passive or unaffected. Jung: "seen from the outside, it looks as though the effect of the object did not penetrate into the subject at all," but a subjective content "intercept[s] the effect of the object" (Ch. 10). Si people are "hyper-sensitive," with "a mimosa-like quality."
- It is not just memory or conservatism. Beebe frames it as reality-verification against an archetypal standard, and it is "an irrational function, which can appreciate the reality of the psyche even against rational considerations, because it trusts the evidence of its own senses" (Ch. 5).
- It is easily confused with Ni and Fi (Ch. 13).

**Si in shadow and stress positions:**
- **Senex Si** (INTP, ISFP). Senex Si "might point out the clumsiness of the son's hand movements" (Ch. 9). Fault-finding about procedure, done in a withering way.
- **Trickster Si** (ENFJ, ENTJ). Toto: "a trickster, who consistently moves the plot forward by creating some kind of mischief that breaks with an established order of things," such as biting Almira Gulch or jumping out of the balloon. The effect is "to subvert an inflated use of power" (Ch. 5).
- **Demonic Si** (INTJ, INFJ). Jung's Anabaptists, an "ungrounded, restless introverted sensation," "invade Jung's space like an attack of indigestion, demanding his immediate help to find the peace they seek, which, rather concretely, they imagine to be located in a specific geographical space." This was the hardest moment in the Red Book, and dealing with it produced Jung's religious attitude (Ch. 6, 11, 12).
- **Opposing Si** (ESTP, ESFP). [derived] Avoidant, foot-dragging inertia, such as "that's not how it's done" used defensively.
- **Inferior Si** (ENTP, ENFP; Beebe's own). The **Chinese laundress** anima: "practical, unadorned... worked very efficiently," left "forlorn and unfurnished" because Beebe neglected his breathing and his body. He learned to breathe while listening to patients, the migraines stopped, and in a later dream "her husband had been taking her out for ice cream!" (Ch. 3).

**Stories and metaphors:**
- **Emma Jung**: "like a highly sensitized photographic plate" (Ch. 12).
- **Hitchcock** used Si cinematically: "the uncanny power of seemingly ordinary images to stimulate unexpected, archetypal reactions" (Ch. 10).
- **Jung's descriptions of the types** in Psychological Types Ch. X are themselves Si work: he observed, compared with memories of similar people, and noted likenesses and differences (Ch. 12).
- **Henderson** on dreams: Ni "perceives the variety and the possibility for development of the inner images, whereas introverted sensation perceives the specific image which defines the psychic activity that needs immediate attention" (Ch. 6).
- **The new Attorney General dream**: Te combined with Si in "planning and accounting for how money was spent" (Ch. 4).

**Key quotes:**
- "Introverted sensation concerns itself primarily with finding order, organizing experience, and monitoring the comfort of the body on the inside" (Ch. 3, Type development).
- "The person who carries introverted sensation as his dominant ego function can generally understand how any process works and by attending to very small details can verify whether the correct procedures are being followed" (Ch. 4, The demonic personality).
- "Introverted sensation likes to establish whether the experience of the object checks out with an inner sense of what has already been established through long human experience as 'real'" (Ch. 13).

### 5. Te: extraverted thinking (planning)

**Core definition.** Te is goal-oriented thinking "intended to be shared with others" (Ch. 12). It looks for "definitions that would hold true for everyone, according to ideas everyone might agree with" (Ch. 3). It goes from regulating to planning to enforcing. It organizes people and events toward objectives and makes established, clear principles the governing rule. At its best it produces principles "everyone soon could follow" (Locke, Mozart; Ch. 10).

**Behavioral markers [derived]:**
1. You naturally make the plan and set the agenda, including for the people helping you ("hurry up, be more concise, get to the point," Ch. 4).
2. In a group, you turn a shared concern into steps, rules and assignments.
3. You prefer simple, clear explanations that everyone can accept over intricate ones ("tends to simple, elegant explanations and shuns complexity," Ch. 12).
4. You apply rules consistently, "regardless of the individual personalities involved" (Ch. 6).
5. You want your ideas out in the world: published, implemented, adopted (Louise N.'s question, Ch. 12).
6. You check new claims against established authority or consensus ("You don't learn from people who know nothing," Ch. 3).
7. You plan out your own life in stages (Ch. 4).
8. You build an argument like a legal brief (Ch. 12).

**What it is NOT / common confusions:**
- It is not the same as Ti. Clinicians don't know "the difference between extraverted and introverted thinking" (Ch. 10). Te defines things by outer agreement; Ti defines them by inner truth (Ch. 3).
- Its weakness is a lack of fresh thinking: it "tends to become enamored of established ideas, frequently neglecting the duty to think freshly... There is no brake, therefore, against insisting that these ideas should govern everyone's behavior" (Ch. 10).
- Being bossy is not the same as having Te in a high position. The bossy version may be the senex (see below).

**Te in shadow and stress positions:**
- **Senex Te** (INTJ, ENTP; Beebe's own). "Unconsciously directive, controlling, and pre-established Te way, telling people rather grossly what their priorities ought to be in a way that can be intrusive and offensive" (Ch. 9). "Dogmatic, donnish extraverted thinking that didn't listen to, or even care about, others' ideas" (Ch. 7). "Thinking is senex when it is dogmatic and no longer heedful of the need in life for fresh starts... one insists with a client on the tried and true, and argues against any move to change the status quo" (Ch. 9).
- **Senex in public life.** The Senate questioning of Anita Hill "regularly voices a depreciative and skeptical, if not frankly cynical, interpretation of nearly anyone's suitability" (Ch. 4).
- **Demonic Te** (ENFJ, ESFJ). The Wizard of Oz's "pompous obfuscations" and "callous opportunism." Held to integrity, the same Te turns daimonic: the Wizard reframes things "like a therapist" with diplomas and medals (Ch. 5–6).
- **Trickster Te** (INFJ, ISFJ). Henry in *Henry Fool* (Ch. 11). [derived] Using rules or procedure to trap someone ("I'd love to, but policy...").
- **Opposing Te** (INTP, ISTP). [derived] Rigid, oppositional rule-citing when your own logic is challenged.
- **Inferior Te** (Fi-dominant types). Freud developed his Te theory after 40 and gave rings to loyal followers, a sign of dogmatism. Kim Novak's animus couldn't "stand up to... a script" (Ch. 8–9).
- **Gaston** in Disney's *Beauty and the Beast*: "always making plans for everyone and is blind to the feelings of others" (Ch. 4).

**Key quotes:**
- "Extraverted thinking such as my father's was interested in definitions that would hold true for everyone, according to ideas everyone might agree with" (Ch. 3).
- "When extraverted thinking is highly differentiated, the analyst will find that it is safe to let the patient set the agenda, like a general directing the campaign of the therapy" (Ch. 13).
- "Extraverted thinking tends to become enamored of established ideas, frequently neglecting the duty to think freshly about what is being expressed and the language that is really appropriate to it" (Ch. 10).

### 6. Ti: introverted thinking (defining)

**Core definition.** Ti defines and names things according to an inner sense of truth, "regardless of what the received opinion might be" (Ch. 3). It "naming things in fresh ways, in relation less to outer definitions already agreed upon than to an archetypal sense of the things' unconscious resonance" (Ch. 8). It goes from naming to defining to understanding. Beebe also calls it "the need to make experience conform to a thinking model held and checked within for 'internal' consistency" (Ch. 13).

**Behavioral markers [derived]:**
1. You accept an idea only "if it was true to my experience," and you rebuild other people's theories in your own terms before you can take them in (Ch. 3).
2. You keep refining a definition after others have lost patience. You "do not know when to stop" (Ch. 10, 13).
3. You help friends by clarifying what they already know, "defin[ing] more sharply," like an editor (Ch. 9).
4. You enjoy building models or diagrams for their own sake ("This is what introverted thinking likes to do!", Ch. 13).
5. Finding the exact right word or name for something matters to you.
6. You'll think a thought even if it is "dangerous, subversive, heretical, or wounding to other people's feelings" (Ch. 10, quoting Jung).
7. When you focus on a problem, people near you can feel shut out or "warded off" (Ch. 10).
8. You are not the one asked to motivate people to follow an existing plan or to set up the party (Ch. 9).

**What it is NOT / common confusions:**
- Being abstract is not the same as preferring Ti. Beebe himself shows a Ti-heavy writing style but is Ne-dominant: "it would be easy for the reader to conclude that I have introverted thinking as my leading function. But, no" (Ch. 13). A strong auxiliary can look like the dominant.
- It is not Te (see above).
- It is not intellectualizing to avoid feeling. Beebe thanks his analyst for not "complaining... that I was intellectualizing" (Ch. 3).

**Ti in shadow and stress positions:**
- **Demonic Ti** (Fi-dominant types; Freud). Shadowy Ti "may choose a name that gives the thing named a disagreeable association." Freud named a normal child's attachment "Oedipus" and normal self-regard "Narcissism," which slowed psychology down for generations (Ch. 8). An Fi-dominant person "tends instinctively to use introverted thinking to undercut himself and others and only occasionally 'gets it right'" (Ch. 4). It can also be "a truly daimonic introverted thinking that undermines all our fictions" (Ch. 6).
- **Senex Ti** (ESTJ, ENTJ). [derived from the Te/Ti logic] Withering, hair-splitting definitional correction.
- **Trickster Ti** (ENFP, ESFP). [derived] Logical loopholes and technicalities used to slip out of binds.
- **Opposing Ti** (ESTJ, ENTJ have Ti in position 5). [derived] Hyper-critical "your logic is flawed" used defensively.
- **Puer Ti** (INFJ, ISFJ, ENFJ? No: Ti is tertiary for INFJ and ISFJ). An Ni-dominant woman with Fe auxiliary became tongue-tied with her lawyer and "died intestate," yet wrote "rather funny poetry." This is "the ability to oscillate between states of abject empty-headedness and over-the-top inventiveness" (Ch. 9). Sugar Cane in *Some Like It Hot*: "I always get the fuzzy end of the lollipop." Jack's puella partner in *Husbands and Wives* has "incessant but undeveloped, adolescent thinking" (Ch. 14).
- **Inferior Ti** (Fe-dominant types). The Scarecrow, "If I Only Had a Brain": an inferiority complex with "comical physical incoordination," yet his Ti integrity names the Wizard "humbug" (Ch. 5).

**Stories and metaphors:**
- **The butcher-knife dream**: thinking as a "capacity to cleave and dissect" (Ch. 3, 14).
- **Freud's fish-nerve research** led to fixation and regression: "only introverted thinking would dig deep enough to find that development can not only get arrested, but that it can sometimes also... regress" (Ch. 12).
- **The professor of medicine** at Sharp's dinner party arrives without his wife, preoccupied with his disease (Ch. 13).
- **Rilke's Apollo**, a gaze that defines "all of what we are" (Ch. 6).
- **Philemon** as wise Ti with a hint of senex (Ch. 11).

**Key quotes:**
- "Introverted thinking such as mine had to reflect on whether a particular construction really accorded with the conviction of inner truth, regardless of what the received opinion might be" (Ch. 3).
- "Defining, clarifying, and enhancing the expression of something so that it is congruent with the thing's inner reality is a Ti (introverted thinking) function" (Ch. 9).
- "Introverted thinking can usually be distinguished by the fact that it tends not to know when to stop, and needs to define everything freshly, to the point that it becomes exhausting and hard to follow" (Ch. 13).

### 7. Fe: extraverted feeling (affirming)

**Core definition.** Fe "seeks to connect with the feelings of others" (Ch. 3). It goes from validating to affirming to relating. It places value on the feelings that are present in a shared situation and adapts to shared values, with "discrimination, empathy, and tact." It "tends to appreciate the strengths of people, but it also seeks concrete gratitude and validation" (Ch. 15). It is "concerned with mutual trust and the harmonious working of groups" (Ch. 6).

**Behavioral markers [derived]:**
1. You put people at ease with "a stream of reassuring words"; your style is "communicative, even chatty" (Ch. 13).
2. You notice and respond to the feelings others are showing right now, and you make them your concern (Dorothy, Ch. 5).
3. People around you feel appreciated, respected, and that "one's good will [is] being seen and met" (Ch. 13).
4. You care whether a choice or a partner is "suitable" by shared standards (Ch. 10, quoting Jung).
5. You feel hurt when your kindness isn't acknowledged with gratitude (Beebe's puer Fe, Ch. 3).
6. You keep a group together and keep morale up (Ch. 6).
7. You avoid moves that would break community norms or embarrass someone publicly (the contrast Beebe draws with Jo's Fi, Ch. 13).
8. In a tense moment you calm things by bringing people into contact ("Hey Nick, C'mon over here," Ch. 2).

**What it is NOT / common confusions:**
- It is not persona or politeness. Hillman: "the persona is a collective way of playing a role in the world; the feeling function is an individual instrument of self-affirmation." Fe "can be highly differentiated, uncollective and original" (Ch. 13).
- It is not the same as being an extravert who cares. That is the Jo Wheelwright conflation of extraverted Ne with Fi caring (Ch. 13).
- Being burdened by others' feelings points away from Fe. "The person who constantly obsesses about small feeling matters, finding other people's feelings an endless burden, may be not an extraverted feeling type... but someone with inferior extraverted feeling, that is, an introverted thinking type" (Ch. 13). This is directly relevant to scoring.
- Being sugary is not differentiated Fe: Osterman's "sugary gambit" with Jung (Ch. 2).

**Fe in shadow and stress positions:**
- **Opposing Fe** (Fi-dominant types; Freud). "Fraught with accusations of disloyalty, and examples of unrecognized unfaithfulness" (Ch. 8). Ilsa is the opposing Fe to Rick's Fi hero in *Casablanca*: argumentative but sexualized (Ch. 4).
- **Trickster Fe** (INTJ, ISTJ). Hitchcock to Kim Novak: "My dear Miss Novak, you can wear anything you want, anything—provided it's what the script calls for" (Ch. 9). The door-to-door salesman: "You're wondering who this stranger could be at your door..." E.T. as Fe trickster (Ch. 11).
- **Demonic Fe** (Ti-dominant types; also America's shadow). Melvin Udall in *As Good as It Gets* uses a "sweet voice" to coax the dog before throwing it down the trash chute. Later, engaged by integrity: "You make me want to be a better man" (Ch. 4). Police Chief Gates is "the smiling, sinister white man... the man with demonic extraverted feeling... a chain of perverse reactions in the name of feeling for others" (Ch. 15). The Disney Beast is Fe "locked into a demonic presentation of his caring" (Ch. 4).
- **Shadow aspect generally** (Ch. 15 box): Fe "tends to discriminate against feelings that are less easy to identify with... ignore or harshly judge emotional needs that do not validate collective norms... bullying and prejudice."
- **Witch/Senex Fe** (ENFP, ESFP). [derived] Guilt-tripping, social freezing-out, or public shaming.
- **Puer Fe** (ENTP, ESTP; Beebe's own). He could "leap to unusual heights of empathic compassion... [and] plunge to the depths of despair when the person... did not show any gratitude" (Ch. 3).

**Stories and metaphors:**
- **The two Italian shoeshine men** (Ch. 2). The extraverted one calms a psychotic man by calling him over, "just to be there for someone else and let that person's own psyche absorb the healing effect of the fellow-feeling."
- **Dorothy** as Fe heroine (Ch. 5).
- **Jack**, "compulsively reassuring" (Ch. 14).
- **The hostess** at Sharp's dinner party (Ch. 13).
- **Von Franz's** "feeling decency" (Ch. 2).

**Key quotes:**
- "Extraverted feeling, by contrast, tends to be more communicative, even chatty, using a stream of reassuring words to put people at ease" (Ch. 13, Parental energy of the auxiliary function).
- "When extraverted feeling is differentiated, the analyst feels appreciated and respected, and there is a sense of one's good will being seen and met" (Ch. 13).
- "The extraverted feeling function concerns itself with other people's emotions—especially those that lie on or near the surface and are easy to sympathize with" (Ch. 15, boxed definition).

### 8. Fi: introverted feeling (appraising)

**Core definition.** Fi measures what happens against an inner, archetypal ideal of what is fitting. It "wants to know if the object as experienced is conducting itself in accord with what is fitting for such an object, that is, if a bride is acting like a bride, if a home feels like a home, if the boss is behaving as she should in her role" (Ch. 13). It goes from judging to appraising to "establishing the value," the one stage Beebe couldn't put in a single word. It is "mainly concerned with the values that matter most to oneself" (Ch. 3). It is "very sensitive to imbalances of power," and its dream symbol is the judge (Ch. 4, 11).

**Behavioral markers [derived]:**
1. Your caring is often wordless and practical, aimed at the underlying problem rather than at soothing words (Jo wiping the piano bench, the anonymous weekly bouquets, Ch. 13).
2. You quickly notice when someone is misusing power or position, and it angers you (the shoeshine man yelling at panhandlers, Ch. 2).
3. You judge people or places against how they should be ("Thank God!" about a friend's choice of analyst, Ch. 13).
4. You often keep your judgments to yourself, "out of courtesy" (Ch. 11), but you can drop social niceties to say a critical verdict outright.
5. You hold back praise or reassurance you'd find insincere (Ch. 15).
6. When a feeling like "bad" takes hold, you need to feel it all the way through before you can move on (Ch. 10).
7. Your gestures of care are unusual, can make people slightly uncomfortable at first, and then win them over (Jo climbing through the waiting-room window, Ch. 13).
8. You hold to your own standard of quality even when no one else would notice (the "deeper shine," Ch. 2).
9. You feel intense disappointment when reality doesn't live up to the ideal (Ch. 15).

**What it is NOT / common confusions:**
- It is not narcissism or a borderline presentation. An Fi-dominant patient "might, even today, be perceived by clinicians trained in the Freudian tradition as pathologically narcissistic or even borderline" (Ch. 12).
- It is not coldness or indifference, though Jung says Fi "assumes an air of profound indifference as a means of defense" (Ch. 10).
- It is not Ni (it "can only feel the archetypal image... cannot see it") and not Fe (it is wordless and "quirkier" rather than reassuring) (Ch. 10, 13).
- It is not depression, though "Perhaps we all get into our introverted feeling when we are depressed" (Ch. 10).

**Fi in shadow and stress positions:**
- **Witch/Senex Fi** (ENFJ, ESFJ, INFJ, ISFJ? Only INFJ and ISFJ have Fi in position 6). Fi in the witch or senex position "can take the form of a cold silence that implies that something someone has just said or done is in such bad taste that the person doing it deserves an unspoken excommunication" (Ch. 9). Judy in *Husbands and Wives*: "Where he comforts and enables, she probes and disrupts, in the manner of an archetypal witch" (Ch. 14).
- **Opposing Fi** (ENFJ, ESFJ). Almira Gulch, "so concerned with protecting her own interests that she doesn't care enough about the feelings of others," is a "deployer of power" (Ch. 5).
- **Trickster Fi** (ENTP, ESTP; Beebe's own). It "did not feel bound by medical and Christian cultural expectations," and let him say "I'm not sure I can work with you if it's going to be this negative," which ended the bullying (Ch. 3).
- **Demonic Fi** (Ti-dominant types). Beebe re-reads Hillman's "inferior feeling" this way: "loaded with anger and rage and ambition and aggression as well as with greed and desire... thousands of tiny, angry resentments... The cat neglected becomes the unconscious tiger" (Ch. 3).
- **Shadow aspect generally** (Ch. 15): Fi "becomes rageful, anxious, and sullen. It may withdraw all support for attitudes it has decided are simply wrong, even at the risk of rupturing relationship."
- **Puer Fi** (INTJ, ISTJ). Jung's Fi was "too pure, too critical of others, and too removed from the give and take of real life." As a third function it is "a less reliable judge... Like a child, it may show delight or turn up its nose" (Ch. 11). Hitchcock's tertiary Fi "could not stand to have conflict on the set" (Ch. 9).

**Stories and metaphors:**
- **The Calabrian shoeshine man** (Ch. 2): he rarely talked, said "This is the best!", and pointed at his sign instead of naming his price. After ten years he said "You are a very nice man." His "introverted feeling... was ever tracking power."
- **The blind men and the elephant**, as Fi feeling its way slowly around the archetype (Ch. 10).
- **Rilke**: "I love you, but of course it's none of your business" (Ch. 10).
- **Rick** in *Casablanca* (Ch. 4); **Belle** (Ch. 4).
- **The Byronic young patient**, whose Fi ego burst out: "it was I who was being pompous" (Ch. 4).
- **Rodney King** and Fi's "bench of judgment" (Ch. 15).

**Key quotes:**
- "Introverted feeling is mainly concerned with the values that matter most to oneself, while extraverted feeling seeks to connect with the feelings of others" (Ch. 3).
- "It is important to realize that, when the introverted feeling function is, for example, feeling 'bad,' it is feeling the entire archetypal category of 'bad' and is not likely to quit until that archetypal badness is felt through" (Ch. 10).
- "Wordless expression is a common feature of introverted feeling. It is more interested in solving an underlying feeling problem than in making communications to address people's expressions of discomfort triggered by the problem" (Ch. 13).

## PART C. Implications for scoring

### C.1 What score pattern to expect, using INFJ as the example (Ni Fe Ti Se | Ne Fi Te Si)

Beebe explicitly rejects a descending 1-to-8 strength gradient, so a correct "fit" pattern for an INFJ is a set of qualitative signatures, not "Ni > Fe > Ti > Se > Ne > ...". The signatures below are [derived] from Beebe's role descriptions.

| Position | INFJ | Expected self-report signature [derived] |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Hero | Ni | High on ease, identity, competence, pleasure, reliability and remembered successes. Used for self-assertion. |
| 2 Parent | Fe | High on "how I help others." Reliable, outward-facing and caring. Often the most visible to others, and can be mistaken for the dominant. |
| 3 Puer | Ti | Uneven: high on some items (creative bursts, enjoying it when no one is watching) and low on items about performing under pressure. Needs support. Possibly anxiety items. |
| 4 Anima | Se | Low on skill and ease. High on embarrassment and shame. Possibly high on values and idealism, for example caring about food quality, the body or beauty (Ch. 8's Ni-dominant example). |
| 5 Opposing | Ne | Moderate, and mostly reactive: contrarian "what ifs" when one's own vision is challenged, avoidance, suspicion. Likely under-reported. |
| 6 Witch | Fi | Moderate, and situational: cold verdicts, freezing someone out, "unspoken excommunication" when someone threatens what one values. |
| 7 Trickster | Te | Low to moderate. Shows up as double-binding through rules, technicalities or deadlines, or as feeling double-bound by other people's rules. |
| 8 Demonic | Si | Usually lowest in conscious endorsement, but with occasional undermining episodes, plus rare "daimonic" moments of unexpected good sense. Others notice it more than the self does. |

**Should shadow functions score low, or medium but defensive?** Beebe's text supports the second answer. The shadow functions are used ("we each actually make use of all the function-attitudes"), they are "ego-dystonic," and they appear "in antagonistic, paradoxical, depreciating, and destructive ways," often outside awareness: "most of us are quite unconscious of the impact on other people of the functions carried by the opposing or demonic personality" (Ch. 8). Practical consequences [derived]:
1. On "I do X" items, shadow functions may come out moderate, not low, especially items about things done under threat or conflict.
2. On "X feels like me / I enjoy X / X energizes me" items, they should come out low.
3. Self-report will under-detect the opposing and demonic functions because they sit in the hero's blind spot. Optional observer-report items would help here.

### C.2 Pitfalls that cause mis-scoring, by Beebe's own account

1. **Inferior-function idealism.** "The higher cause or mission that seizes our energy is often associated with this area of the psyche where we are ourselves rather weak and inept" (Ch. 8). An INTP may strongly endorse "group harmony matters deeply" (Fe items), and an ISFP may love abstruse philosophy (Te). Value-endorsement items will inflate the inferior function. Measure capability, ease and energy separately from valuing.
2. **Burden versus ease.** Obsessing over feelings and finding them a burden signals inferior Fe, not Fe preference (Ch. 13). Items like "I think a lot about other people's feelings" are ambiguous. Pair them with "...and it comes easily" or "...and it exhausts me."
3. **Conflation of the top two.** People combine the attitude of the dominant with the function of the auxiliary, for example reading ENFP (Ne + Fi) as "Fe" (Ch. 13). Score function and attitude separately at item level, and don't let general extraversion items load onto every extraverted function.
4. **A strong auxiliary looks dominant.** Beebe's Ti-heavy prose comes from an Ne dominant (Ch. 13). Separate self-assertion items (hero) from caring-for-others items (parent).
5. **Persona and falsification.** Conforming to "the expectations of a family, school, occupation, or culture" produces a "falsification of the original personality" (Ch. 13). Jung's own Te "as if it were a superior function was 'a falsification of type'" (Ch. 11). Add items that separate what work requires of you from what you'd do if free.
6. **Complex states.** Under stress (an "abaissement du niveau mental"), the tertiary and inferior functions surface "floridly neurotic" (Ch. 13). Ask respondents to answer from relaxed, authentic moments, or flag answers that describe a stress state.
7. **The introverted functions blur together.** Fi, Ni and Si are "easily confused with each other" (Ch. 13). Build contrast items that put them side by side. Fi asks whether this is fitting; Ni asks what image or pattern it is; Si asks whether it checks out with established reality and my body.
8. **Same function, opposite attitude.** The shadow pairs (Fe/Fi, Ni/Ne and so on) are the hardest to tell apart. Forced-choice items between attitude-opposites of one function are the most diagnostic.

### C.3 How a test can separate a preferred function from a shadow one, using Beebe's distinctions

- **Ownership.** Ego-syntonic functions are "what I normally own as part of 'me' and 'my' values." Shadow functions are "incompatible with my conscious ego" (Ch. 7). Item stems: "When I do this, it feels like the real me" versus "I sometimes catch myself doing this and don't like it."
- **Trigger context.** The opposing function "lurch[es] forward first when we feel our heroic superior function and its most cherished values to be under attack" (Ch. 8). The senex appears when someone or something seems "fundamentally destructive and dangerous to the things we value" (Ch. 9). Item stems: "When someone challenges my view, I tend to..." versus "On an ordinary good day, I tend to..."
- **Purpose.** Shadow archetypes "serve not to realize the aims of the personality, but to defend it, usually by managing other people in oppositional and underhanded ways" (Ch. 8). Ask whether the behavior aims to create something or to defend yourself.
- **Enjoyment and energy.** "The exercise and performance of a function is something to enjoy" (Hillman, Ch. 13). Beebe: "This is what introverted thinking likes to do!" Discovering his type "gave me a lot of energy" (Ch. 3).
- **Role signatures:**
  - Hero: competence plus pleasure plus reliability plus recalling past wins.
  - Parent: how you care for others. "It is often easier to type the way they would ideally like to take care of another person than... what they want for themselves" (Ch. 9).
  - Puer: swings between inflation and deflation, freezes under social demand, creative when unpressured.
  - Anima: shame, an "agony" to use, a "yelp of complaint," high ideals, growth after midlife.
- **Other people's reactions.** Feedback you experience as "unfair" often comes from your shadow: "'That person has seen my shadow'" (Ch. 8). Items like "People sometimes accuse me of ___ even though I don't see it" can flag the opposing and demonic functions.
- **Wait for the authentic gift.** "It is better to wait until the patient shows an original gift for accurately construing or managing some aspect of what comes up... rather than attempt to 'type' the person when he or she is manifesting a collective persona" (Ch. 13). For a questionnaire: anchor items in peak, natural moments.

### C.4 Design ideas that go beyond item scoring [derived]

- **Report axes, not only single functions.** Beebe judges compatibility by the spine, not by J/P: "I prefer to look at each individual's vertical axis, or spine of consciousness" (Ch. 3). Berens and Montoya "look at whole axes" (Ch. 10). Fit each of the 16 eight-position templates to the respondent's profile and report the best fit, plus the runner-up.
- **Results storytelling.** Present the eight positions as a "cast of characters" (Ch. 7), using film examples such as Oz for ENFJ, *Husbands and Wives* for INFJ, and Hitchcock for ISTJ. Explain why each function scored as it did by naming the archetypal role, for example: "Your Fi scored moderate because it appears mostly as cold verdicts when values feel threatened. That is the witch role."
- **Three development tiers.** Use the Ch. 1 keyword tiers (persona, heart, goal) as a "how developed is this function" indicator.

## PART D. Beebe's caveats on dynamism, development, spectrum and boxing people

- **Not rigid.** "I say this lest anyone reading what I have written about the archetypes in this book take the eight-function, eight-archetype model as an unaltering image of inevitable rigidity... The reader is therefore advised to approach the book with this developmental perspective in mind" (Preface).
- **Types of consciousness, not types of people.** "It has not always been clear to students of Jung's analytical psychology what his famous 'types' are types of. The commonest assumption has been that they refer to types of people. But for Jung, they were types of consciousness" (Ch. 10, Introduction).
- **Jung's Argentine foreword.** Type theory "should be used not as a way of classifying people but for 'sorting out the empirical material'" (Ch. 3). Similarly, "In this spirit of identifying typical processes rather than typical people" (Ch. 14).
- **No fixed hierarchy.** "I no longer view the type profile of an individual as expressing a hierarchy of differentiation of the various functions of consciousness" (Ch. 7). The numbers are "qualitative rather than quantitative" (Ch. 9).
- **Nature and history both shape type.** Positions come "partly from a hard-wired typological hierarchy (one's innate 'type') and partly as a consequence of one's developmental history of reward and trauma in family and culture" (Ch. 14).
- **Lifelong development.** "In reality, type remains an issue throughout the individuation process." Assuming type development ends with finding the inferior function "can actually interfere with the development of consciousness" (Ch. 3). Beebe admits that "my own perspective on the subject is continuing to develop" (Preface).
- **Developmental timing.** The dominant is stable from early childhood. "The tertiary and inferior functions normally do not appear until adulthood" (Murphy, cited in Ch. 3 and 10). The anima or animus develops in the second half of life (Ch. 8).
- **Situations call for functions.** "Life itself presents problems that are already differentiated in such a way that only a particular function of consciousness can solve them... the development of consciousness involves the ability to summon the various functions at appropriate times in appropriate ways" (Ch. 3). "Effective living requires, sooner or later, that we use all our function-attitudes" (Ch. 9).
- **Introversion and extraversion as a continuum.** Beebe's first analyst "had said it was a 'continuum.'" Half his friends saw him as an extravert and half as "the only true introvert they knew" (Ch. 3).
- **Self-discovery beats being told.** "Only some such direct experience of the types as one's own, and the permission to consider them in one's own way, can enable a patient to avail himself or herself of the individuation potential of the type theory. Otherwise, type becomes another way to learn from others what one is" (Ch. 3).
- **Caution in typing.** "We should be cautious, therefore, in making any type diagnosis. It is best not to try to type someone who has not yet made a connection to the self that would be natural to him or her" (Ch. 13). Typing is a "knack" (Wheelwright, Ch. 13).
- **Don't pathologize.** "To be unaware of the types is to risk unnecessarily pathologizing what may actually be adaptive and healthy" (Ch. 12). "Simply not pathologizing introversion is perhaps the most healing thing a therapist or type counselor can do" (Ch. 13).
- **The model's limits.** It describes good type development and "does not account for the many falsifications of type" (Ch. 3). Work on the shadow "has to be tentative, because we never fully see our own shadow" (Ch. 7).
- **Integration.** The aim is to stay with oneself "drawing upon all of the archetypes that can make this possible, but without being trapped in any of them" (Ch. 4). The "stretch" into a non-preferred function makes us "more open to other non-preferred types of consciousness too" (Ch. 6).
- **Ethics.** "Gradually, perhaps, consciousness will realize its potential to become conscience" (Ch. 3).

## PART E. Type tests and their limits (mostly Ch. 10, plus Ch. 3 and 13)

- **The instruments.** Beebe covers the Gray–Wheelwright Jungian Type Survey (1940s, the first paper-and-pencil test), the MBTI, the MMTIC for children, and the Singer–Loomis Inventory (SLIP, 1980). The SLIP is the one that scores each of the eight function-attitudes separately instead of using forced choices.
- **The bipolar assumption.** All the forced-choice instruments are built on Jung's "bipolar assumption" that we cannot think and feel, or take in sensation and use intuition, at the same time.
- **The 1994 comparison** (Karesh, Pieper & Holland, quoted in Ch. 10): MBTI and JTS agreed substantially on E–I, moderately on S–N, and only to a limited extent on T–F. "Evidence was equivocal for the instruments' ability to indicate dominant function." "The SLIP measures different constructs than either the MBTI or the JTS, so that little support was found for Singer and Loomis' challenge to Jung's bipolarity assumption."
  - [derived] Relevance for this site: a sakinorva-style test that scores each function independently faces the same validity question as the SLIP. Including some forced-choice contrasts between opposite functions and opposite attitudes would help.
- **MBTI ethics.** The Association for Psychological Type "considers it unethical to type someone simply on the basis of their results on the MBTI, which is after all only an 'Indicator.'" There must be a follow-up along the lines of "this is the type the instrument indicates, and you can see if it really fits you." Annie Murphy Paul (2004) criticized using it to assign identities (Ch. 10).
- **The respondent is the final judge.** "It is a core feeling value in MBTI facilitation that each person should be the final judge of who they fundamentally are" (Ch. 13). Beebe links this to Myers's own Fi.
- **J/P only describes the leading extraverted function.** Myers had to infer rational versus irrational from that function because her instrument "focused on easily identifiable behaviors in the outer world" (Ch. 3).
- **The attitude-alternation debates.** Wheelwright held that functions 1 to 3 share the same attitude; Spoto dissented on the third function. Everyone agrees the inferior takes the opposite attitude to the dominant (Ch. 10, 13). Tests built on different theories will disagree about the tertiary.
- **Beebe's own test results matched his self-analysis.** MBTI (1968) and JTS both gave ENTP or extraverted intuitive-thinking, but the JTS framework assumed both of his leading functions were extraverted (Ch. 3). The same answers can produce different type readings depending on the model.
- **Practitioners lean on questionnaires.** Plaut (1972) found fewer than half of Jungian analysts use type at all, and those who do "often rely on questionnaire results rather than clinical observation" (Ch. 10).
- **Recognition is the real bottleneck.** "Very few us, whether clients or type practitioners, can actually recognize the eight function-attitudes" (Ch. 1). Beebe's everyday-language keywords exist to fix this. That supports the plan for transparent, story-based result explanations.
