Extraverted feeling is often mistaken for niceness, or for being emotional. It is neither. It is a rational, outward-facing way of weighing what matters, through the relationships, customs and shared values that hold people together.

## In one breath

Fe evaluates by relationship: who is this to me, what does this moment call for, how will this land with the people here? It reads and uses the shared language of gestures, rituals and courtesies, and it is energised when people get along.

> For Feeling types, talking is doing.
> — Lenore Thomson, *Personality Type*, p. 331

## How it works

Jung described extraverted feeling as oriented by objective data: it agrees with shared values. His example is famous: someone calls a host's painting "beautiful", not as a lie but as an act of accommodation that keeps the feeling atmosphere harmonious. Without this function, he wrote, a beautiful and harmonious sociability would be unthinkable.

Thomson's clearest example: a friend's basement floods. To the shop assistant who mentions it, you offer sympathy. For your best friend, you bring a mop and a bucket. Same event, different relationship, different right response. Fe sorts its obligations by kind of relationship, family, friend, colleague, and acts accordingly, sometimes overriding personal liking for the sake of what the role requires.

Beebe's keywords: validating, *affirming*, and at its most developed, relating. He stresses that differentiated Fe can be highly original and individual; it is not the same as social persona.

## In everyday life

- In a group you pick up on the mood and adjust what you say.
- You work out what you think by talking it through with others.
- You remember the gestures that matter: birthdays, thank-yous, check-ins.
- You notice when someone is left out and bring them in.
- Saying no is hard, and you can become overstretched.
- When people around you get along, you feel lighter.

Myers adds that extraverted feeling types concentrate on people's most admirable qualities, and have faith that harmony can be achieved, often managing to bring it about.

## What it is not

**Not emotion.** Thomson goes so far as to say that the more someone relies on Fe, the *less* in touch with their own feelings they may be, because attention is on the shared situation.

**Not fakeness.** Jung insisted Fe's judgments are genuine.

**Not introverted feeling.** Fe adapts to shared values; Fi measures against private ones. Myers puts it sharply: faced with an unpleasant fact, extraverted feeling tends to deny its existence, introverted feeling condemns it.

**Not the burden of other people's feelings.** Beebe notes that someone who finds others' emotions an endless burden may actually have Fe as an *inferior* function.

## When it leads

ESFJs and ENFJs lead with Fe. Myers describes them as valuing harmonious contact above all, expressing the feeling appropriate to the moment, warmed by approval and sensitive to indifference. Thomson describes ESFJs as "car-poolers" and ENFJs as "road rangers", and notes the sacramental care they put into rituals.

For ISFJs and INFJs, Fe is the helper that brings their inner experience or vision into warm relationship. Myers notes that INFJs can seem extraverted precisely because their feeling is.

## When it is the weakest note

For ISTPs and INTPs, Fe is the inferior. Thomson describes an unacknowledged hunger for approval leading to a growing sense of being unappreciated, sudden life-changing decisions when a relationship "feels right", and getting tangled in public disputes. Jung described it as a primitive feeling to which objects attach themselves with magical force.

## In the shadow

Beebe's example of shadow Fe is chilling: a character who uses a sweet, coaxing voice before doing something cruel. As a trickster, Fe uses charm or social pressure to win advantage. Thomson notes ENTPs who strive for support by displaying relatedness tactically, and are badly offended when the gesture fails.

## Growing it

If Fe is quiet for you, try noticing how the other person is feeling in your next conversation before you share your view, and name one small appreciation out loud. Myers' description of feeling used well is "considering what matters most to oneself and others".
