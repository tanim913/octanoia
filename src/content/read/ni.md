Introverted intuition is the most misunderstood of the eight, partly because it is hard to describe from the inside. People who lead with it often struggle to explain how they know what they know. Beebe calls it the function most consistently devalued in contemporary Western culture.

## In one breath

Ni attends to the background of the mind, where images, patterns and meanings form. It reframes problems instead of solving them as given, and its insights tend to arrive whole, carrying conviction but no visible steps.

> Introverted Intuitions are not really ideas. They're like trains at the edge of articulated knowledge.
> — Lenore Thomson, *Personality Type*, p. 229

## How it works

Jung wrote that Ni receives its impetus from outer objects but is never arrested by them; it stays with what the object releases within. It moves from image to image, perceiving the processes in the background of consciousness.

Thomson's account is more down to earth and very useful. Ni notices that the same information can be framed many ways, and that labels and categories decide what we see. It works at the edge of existing language and pushes past it. Her example: at the beach, one friend sunbathes, one packs extra towels, one imagines an amusement park, and the Ni friend asks why a tan became attractive in the first place.

Beebe's keywords: imagining, *knowing*, and at its most developed, divining, both seeing where the future is bending and sensing the purpose hidden in a developing situation.

## In everyday life

- You solve a dilemma by redefining the question rather than choosing within it.
- An image or metaphor arrives that sums up a whole situation.
- You have a strong sense of where something is heading but struggle to list the evidence.
- You lose interest in things once they become obvious or popular.
- You notice how words and labels shape the way people see something.
- Practical upkeep, bills, the dentist, slips while you are absorbed in an idea.

Myers adds that introverted intuitives cannot be successfully coerced. They will not even be *told* anything without permission, but they will accept an offer of facts or theories for free consideration.

## What it is not

**Not psychic powers.** Thomson notes that popular culture stereotypes Ni-led people as mystics or absent-minded professors. Ni is a pattern-sense, and it can be wrong.

**Not introverted feeling.** Beebe draws the line clearly: Ni *sees* the image behind a situation; Fi can only *feel* it.

**Not introverted sensing.** Jung's example: Si attends precisely to a sensation; Ni looks past it to what it signifies.

## When it leads

INTJs and INFJs lead with Ni. Myers called them people who are willing to concede that the impossible takes a little longer, but not much. Thomson describes INTJs who use a theory if it works but don't believe it, and INFJs who seem more outgoing than they are because their helper is extraverted feeling. Jung thought this type the hardest to understand, and also the source of prophets and artists.

For ENTJs and ENFJs, Ni is the helper that gives direction and depth to their outward leadership.

## When it is the weakest note

For ESTPs and ESFPs, Ni is the inferior. Thomson describes it as feeling like criticism from someone "who hasn't been there", and under strain it can surface as vague foreboding, magical thinking or suspicion of conspiracies. Beebe tells of a man led by introverted sensing who heard an inner voice saying "You're nothing", a global, unappealable judgment, until he faced it and it became the push to change careers.

## In the shadow

Ni as an opposing personality can make someone detached and avoidant, "tuning out" when feelings run high. Beebe recognised this in himself. As a trickster it borrows sweeping, spiritual-sounding arguments to win a point.

## Growing it

If Ni is quiet for you, try this after an important event: ask what it might be the beginning of, and let the answer come slowly, without forcing it. Myers' caution applies here too: intuition's temptation is to assume there are no more facts to know, or that you already know them all.
