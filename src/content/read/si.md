Introverted sensing is often reduced to "good memory" or "likes tradition". Both miss what is distinctive about it. Si is a way of perceiving the world through a rich, personal archive of impressions, and of steadying each new experience by comparing it with what has proven real.

## In one breath

Si notices how things actually feel, inside the body and in lived experience, and checks the new against a stored, selective record of what it has known. It cares whether something checks out.

> When we use Introverted Sensation, we stabilize our immediate sense impressions by integrating them with the ones we remember and care about. We "find ourselves" in whatever is happening.
> — Lenore Thomson, *Personality Type*, p. 170

## How it works

Jung's description is surprisingly poetic. Introverted sensation sees the same things as everyone else, but it never stops at the objective effect; it responds to the subjective impression the object releases. He compared it to painters rendering the same landscape in entirely different colours, and to seeing things "as a million-year-old consciousness might see them".

Beebe frames Si as *verifying*: checking whether experience matches an inner sense of what has been established as real, step by step, including monitoring the comfort of the body from the inside. His keywords are implementing, verifying and, at its most developed, accounting.

Thomson stresses selectivity. Five people leaving the same film remember different things, because Si keeps what mattered to *you*, not what was objectively there. Those personal facts become your self-experience.

## In everyday life

- You notice straight away when something has been moved or changed.
- Your body's signals, tiredness, hunger, temperature, noise, strongly shape your day.
- You read documents carefully, sometimes more than once, to be sure.
- Before deciding, you compare the situation with times you have been through before.
- You build deep, sometimes offbeat expertise or collections over years.
- You keep commitments even when circumstances change and no one would blame you for backing out.

Myers adds a delightful observation: introverted sensing types are outwardly matter-of-fact and inwardly entertained by highly individual reactions to their sense impressions, "absurd, irreverent, touching or hilarious, but never predictable".

## What it is not

**Not conservatism.** Thomson insists that Si-led people can hold very firm ideas about "how things should be", and some are diehard reformers. The key is that they hold their views steadily, measured against their own experience.

**Not passivity.** Jung warned that the calm exterior hides intense sensitivity. Beebe calls it "a mimosa-like quality".

**Not introverted intuition.** Jung's sharpest contrast: during a bout of dizziness, Si attends to the precise quality, intensity and course of the sensation; Ni looks behind the scenes and sees an image, a tottering man pierced by an arrow.

## When it leads

ISTJs and ISFJs lead with Si. Myers described them as systematic, painstaking and thorough, hard to distract once committed, and quietly loyal. Thomson's ISJs are masters of gradual, almost imperceptible change: "the very proof of one's priorities is maintaining them when no one expects it of you."

For ESTJs and ESFJs, Si is the helper that grounds their outward organising in what has actually worked.

## When it is the weakest note

For ENTPs and ENFPs, Si is the inferior. Beebe, an ENTP, describes dreaming of a practical, overworked Chinese laundress, an image of the body he neglected while chasing ideas. When he learned to breathe and pay attention to his body while listening to patients, his migraines stopped.

Under strain, inferior Si can show up as the body suddenly "crashing" after long neglect, or as an anxious craving for strict routine and unconditional stability. Thomson describes ENFPs who reach midlife suddenly aware of time passing, yearning for something lasting.

## In the shadow

Si in shadow roles can be fault-finding and withering about procedure, like a critical parent pointing out clumsy hands. As a trickster it creates small mischief that breaks an inflated order: Beebe notes that Toto, the dog in *The Wizard of Oz*, keeps moving the plot along this way.

## Growing it

If Si is quiet for you, try keeping a short note of what worked last time before starting something similar again, and notice your body at regular moments in the day. Myers' advice for sensing in general applies: it is the function for seeing and facing the facts, and its gift is soundness rather than speed.
