Introverted thinking needs things to make sense from the inside. It is not satisfied by an explanation that works, or that experts agree on; it wants to rebuild the logic itself and feel it click.

## In one breath

Ti checks ideas against an internal model of how things really work. It defines, names and refines until everything hangs together. In practical settings it works as tacit, body-level reasoning, weighing many variables at once without spelling them out.

> I don't care what the truth turns out to be, but I want it to hang together.
> — A 17-year-old introverted thinker, quoted by Isabel Myers, *Gifts Differing*, p. 65

## How it works

Jung wrote that introverted thinking begins in the subject and returns to the subject. It formulates questions, creates theories and opens up prospects, but in the presence of facts it is reserved: facts are collected as evidence for an idea, never for their own sake. "Intensity is his aim, not extensity."

Thomson adds a surprising, important dimension. Ti is not only abstract theory. It is the logic behind skilled action: packing groceries by eye, rounding second base and deciding in a flash whether to run. People strong in practical Ti often believe they are using reflexes when they are actually using reason. That is why mechanics, athletes and musicians can be strongly Ti without ever thinking of themselves as "logical".

Beebe's keywords: naming, *defining*, and at its most developed, understanding. He also notes Ti's tell: it does not know when to stop, and needs to define everything freshly.

## In everyday life

- Before accepting an explanation, you rebuild it in your own terms.
- You keep refining a definition after others are satisfied.
- A diagram of the whole system helps more than step-by-step instructions.
- You care whether an idea is consistent more than whether it is popular.
- Spotting an inconsistency gives you a small spark of satisfaction.
- It can be hard to explain how you knew something; it just fit.

## What it is not

**Not "being abstract".** Jung said introverted thinking can work with concrete or abstract material alike.

**Not every clever idea.** Myers warned that much ingenious mental activity is actually intuition. Being quick with ideas is not the same as Ti.

**Not extraverted thinking.** Ti doesn't need agreement; Te does.

## When it leads

ISTPs and INTPs lead with Ti. Myers describes them as using thinking to analyse the world, not to run it: detachedly curious and adaptable until a ruling principle is violated, at which point they stop adapting. Thomson argues that ISTPs and INTPs share this core even though they look very different on the surface, against the popular habit of grouping ISTPs with the action-loving ESTPs.

For ESTPs and ENTPs, Ti is the helper that gives their outward energy a precise inner logic.

## When it is the weakest note

For ESFJs and ENFJs, Ti is the inferior. Thomson describes it surfacing as an impassive, analytical coldness they find almost inhuman, then projecting it as other people's "poor judgment", setting rules to guard everyone against danger, and keeping score of their own sacrifices. Beebe's image is the Scarecrow in *The Wizard of Oz*, sure he has no brain, who nevertheless names the Wizard a humbug.

## In the shadow

Ti in a saboteur role can name things in ways that undercut them. Beebe argues that Freud, Fi-led, did this when he named a normal child's attachment "Oedipus". As a critical parent it becomes withering, hair-splitting correction.

## Growing it

If Ti is quiet for you, take one belief you hold and try to explain, in your own words, exactly why it is true, as if to a sceptical friend. Thomson's insight is that Ti is often already working in your hands; growth means noticing and trusting it.
