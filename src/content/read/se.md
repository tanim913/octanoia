Extraverted sensing is the mind meeting the world at full volume. It takes in what is actually here, now: the texture of a surface, the mood of a room, the speed of a ball coming towards you. And it responds in real time, adjusting as the situation changes.

## In one breath

Se is direct, embodied contact with present reality. It learns by doing, judges how things are going by what it can see and feel happening, and only trusts something as real once it has been touched, tried or tasted.

> Extraverted sensation … is the difference between talking about it and getting to know it intimately. It involves touching and being touched by every part of it.
> — John Beebe, *Energies and Patterns*, ch. 1

## How it works

Jung described Se as the function whose only measure of value is the strength of the sensation itself. The most vivid stimulus wins its attention. Nothing equals it in realism; it sees things as they are, not as they should be or could be made to be.

Thomson adds something subtle. Se is not passive noticing. It is a loop between perceiving and acting, like a mechanic working on an engine: the material changes, the hands change with it, and thought and action move together. Kneading bread, moving a ball down the court, playing in a band: whenever your actions are changing in step with your surface perceptions, you are using Se.

Beebe gives it three stages. On the surface it is *engaging*. At its heart it is *experiencing*. At its most developed it becomes *enjoying*: not hedonism, but a full, almost spiritual appreciation of the actual.

## In everyday life

- You learn a sport, a dish or an instrument by jumping in and repeating it until it is felt, and drop the instructions once you have it.
- In a crisis you respond quickly and practically to what is in front of you.
- You read how a conversation is going from faces and reactions, and might tease to get a response.
- Quality matters to you: good food, good fabric, a great view.
- Once a situation is "in hand", your interest moves on to the next challenge.
- Abstract explanation without something to touch feels thin.

Myers noticed that extraverted sensing types adapt rather than fight the facts. If one way is blocked, they do it another way; they will follow no plan that has stopped fitting the circumstances.

## What it is not

**Not thrill-seeking.** Jung was clear that an Se type may refine sensation to the finest pitch of aesthetic purity. A connoisseur, a surgeon and a jazz drummer all show Se at its best.

**Not the same as introverted sensing.** Thomson calls them mirror images. Se *loses itself* in what is happening; Si *finds itself* in it, anchored by what it already knows. Beebe heard a colleague ask why they were even both called sensation.

**Not shallow.** Beebe describes people led by Se who could not follow his abstract reasoning, not because they lacked depth but because it was too disembodied to be real to them.

## When it leads

ESTPs and ESFPs lead with Se. Thomson describes them as living "right now: explosive, impulsive, kinesthetic". They become the image others admire and imitate. Myers pictured them as the people who make a better showing in life than in school, who pull conflicting factions together, and who refuse to sacrifice present enjoyment to an abstract future.

For ISTPs and ISFPs, Se is the helper that brings their inner logic or inner values out into skilful action.

## When it is the weakest note

For INTJs and INFJs, whose leading function is introverted intuition, Se is the inferior. It tends to happen to them rather than be directed. Thomson tells the story of Maggie, an INTJ who tried to "develop her sensing" by fighting for seats on the morning train with her briefcase, and came home feeling as if she had been in a prizefight.

Under strain, inferior Se can show up as sudden overindulgence in food, shopping or screens, as fixation on physical details, or as an anxious need to possess. Jung described it as impulsive, unrestrained sensation of a rather primitive kind. Yet Beebe notes that this weak spot often carries the person's ideals: the people who care most passionately about the quality of food may be exactly those with Se in the inferior position.

## In the shadow

Where Se appears as a shadow function, it tends to act bluntly or at the wrong moment. Beebe, an ENTP, had Se in his "daimonic" position: his body language often said the opposite of what he meant, and on an unfamiliar route the opposite of where he thought he should go was almost always correct. In his witch role, Se might bang on the wall at a noisy neighbour.

## Growing it

If Se is quiet for you, practise doing one ordinary thing fully: cooking, walking, a sport, attending only to what your senses report. Myers pointed out that sensing used well is simply "seeing and facing the facts", and that its misuse is running away from a problem into a trivial amusement. The difference is presence.
