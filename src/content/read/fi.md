Introverted feeling is quiet from the outside and intense on the inside. It rarely announces its values, but when one of them is touched, it can be immovable.

## In one breath

Fi measures what happens against an inner ideal shaped by lived experience: is this fitting, is this true to what matters? It puts human value ahead of status, structure or convenience, and it often shows care through wordless, practical gestures.

> They wear their warm side inside, like a fur-lined coat.
> — Isabel Myers, *Gifts Differing*, p. 96

## How it works

Jung described introverted feeling as guided by an image that has no existence in reality but of which it has had a sort of previous vision. It is intensive rather than extensive: deep rather than widely shown. To a superficial observer it can look cold, because its sympathy does nothing visible.

Thomson's image is cooking without a recipe: tasting and adjusting toward a flavour you know in your bones is right. Fi is a lens ground by personal experience and adjusted for present conditions. It "bypasses structural considerations and puts human value first". Her favourite historical example is the buffalo soldiers who, in 1880, arrested Texas Rangers for abusing others, defying status in the name of what was right.

Beebe adds that Fi is very sensitive to imbalances of power, and that when it feels something as "bad", it feels the whole archetypal category of bad and won't quit until it is felt through. His keywords: judging, *appraising*, and at its most developed, establishing the value.

## In everyday life

- You are easygoing about most things, but immovable on a few.
- When something feels wrong, you know it clearly, even if you can't explain why.
- You'd rather show care through a quiet, practical gesture than through words.
- Work you don't believe in drains you, even if it pays well.
- You hold back praise you don't really feel.
- You need time alone to work out how you really feel about something.

Myers describes introverted feeling types as having no desire to impress or influence others, and as open-minded and adaptable until something they value deeply is in danger, at which point they stop adapting.

## What it is not

**Not selfishness.** Jung explicitly rejected calling it egocentric; its values are often deeply humane.

**Not emotional display.** Fi frequently looks calm.

**Not introverted intuition.** Fi feels the image behind a situation; Ni sees it.

**Not a basis for imposing rules on others.** Thomson notes that Fi gains its prophetic power only in relation to a shared framework; on its own it cannot build a social system.

## When it leads

ISFPs and INFPs lead with Fi. Myers calls ISFPs the most modest type and notes that both often prefer the written word or craft to direct self-display. Thomson describes them as feeling they are "making do" until their real life or purpose begins, and drawn to work that crosses boundaries for humane ends.

For ESFPs and ENFPs, Fi is the helper that gives their outward energy an inner compass.

## When it is the weakest note

For ESTJs and ENTJs, Fi is the inferior. Thomson describes it surfacing as the feeling that others aren't being responsible enough, forcing them to "be the grown-up"; as a righteous, lonely stand for justice; or as feeling controlled by other people's needs. Jung described a highly personal, oversensitive feeling that misreads objective opposition as personal ill will.

## In the shadow

Fi as a critical parent can take the form of a cold silence that implies someone has committed an unforgivable lapse of taste. As a saboteur it can withdraw all support from an attitude it has decided is simply wrong, even at the cost of the relationship.

## Growing it

If Fi is quiet for you, try a pause before agreeing to something: does this sit right with me, apart from what anyone expects? Myers' advice for feeling generally applies: it is the function for considering what matters most, and its misuse is rehearsing how right and blameless one has been all along.
