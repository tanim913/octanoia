Most people meet the words *extravert* and *introvert* as a description of sociability: the life of the party versus the quiet one in the corner. Jung meant something more interesting, and once you see it, a lot of confusing type talk falls into place.

## Where the deciding weight comes from

Jung's test was not how much someone talks. It was where the *deciding factor* lives. For the extravert, he wrote, the most frequent and essential decisions are made by objective relations: the situation, the facts, what others expect, what is generally agreed. For the introvert, the subject is the chief factor. The outer situation is noticed, but something inside, a model, an impression, a value, a vision, has the final say.

> Introverted consciousness doubtless views the external conditions, but it selects the subjective determinants as the decisive ones.
> — Jung, *Psychological Types*, p. 472

He gave a homely example. One man puts on a coat because it is cold. Another does not, because he wants to toughen himself up. One admires a famous tenor because everybody does; another withholds admiration precisely because general approval proves nothing. Both pairs see the same facts. They differ in what they let decide.

## Merging and matching

John Beebe offers a beautiful way to feel the difference. Extraversion, he says, reaches out to *merge* with the object: to join the shared experience. Introversion steps back to see whether the experience *matches* an archetype held inside: an inner idea of what this kind of thing is supposed to be.

That is why introverted functions are prone to a particular kind of disappointment. When the world falls short of the inner image, they register it. Beebe insists this is not a disorder; it is simply how introverted judgment and perception work.

## Not the same as outgoing or shy

Because the attitudes describe where decisions come from, a quiet person can lead with an extraverted function, and a talkative person can lead with an introverted one. Lenore Thomson describes introverts who seem entirely extraverted in congenial company, and Beebe recalls that half his friends thought him an extravert and half thought him the only true introvert they knew.

Myers adds an important twist. In an extravert, the leading function is on display; in an introvert, it works inside, and what others meet is the helper.

> In the extravert the General is always out in the open. In the introvert the General is inside the tent … It is the Aide whom others meet.
> — Isabel Myers, *Gifts Differing*, p. 13

So if you have been told you seem extraverted but you feel otherwise, both may be true: people may be meeting your helper, while your leader works out of sight.

## Every function, both directions

The attitudes apply to each function separately. That is why Octanoia scores eight notes instead of four letters. Introverted thinking and extraverted thinking are both logical, but they test truth against different things: an inner model in one case, outside evidence and shared standards in the other. Extraverted feeling and introverted feeling both weigh values, but one tunes to the people and customs around it and the other to a private ideal.

Marie-Louise von Franz, quoted by Beebe, made a striking observation: the hardest person to understand is not your opposite type, but the person with your same function in the other attitude. Two thinkers who test truth in opposite places can find each other baffling in a way a thinker and a feeler rarely do.

## No one lives at one end

Jung was firm that both attitudes live in everyone. A typical attitude only means one predominates. Under ordinary circumstances, he thought, a rhythm of turning outward and inward is part of a healthy life.

> There can never occur a pure type in the sense that he is entirely possessed of the one mechanism with a complete atrophy of the other.
> — Jung, *Psychological Types*, p. 13

When you read your Octanoia results, think of the attitude of each note as a direction of attention, not a personality label. The question is never "are you an introvert?" but "where does this part of your mind look for its answers?"
