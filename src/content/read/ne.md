Extraverted intuition looks at what is in front of it and sees what it could become. A conversation, a vacant shop, a half-formed plan, a stranger with an odd talent: for Ne, each is a doorway.

## In one breath

Ne perceives possibilities in the outer world. From a few cues it grasps a whole pattern and leaps to options that do not yet exist. Once a possibility is pinned down and realised, its attention moves on to the next.

> A fact is acknowledged only in so far as it opens up fresh possibilities of advancing beyond it.
> — Jung, *Psychological Types*, p. 464

## How it works

Thomson describes Ne as right-brain pattern recognition aimed at context: the way you recognise a tune from three notes or a face from a glance. It feels like knowledge even when it is wrong. The extraverted intuitive does have sensations, Jung says, but uses them as springboards for a distant vision rather than as ends in themselves.

Beebe's metaphor is a traffic light only you can see: green means develop it now, red means stop, yellow means proceed carefully. Other people cannot see your signal, so your timing can look arbitrary to them. His keywords for Ne are entertaining (in both senses: amusing people and entertaining ideas), envisioning, and at its best, *enabling* others to see and use the possibilities.

## In everyday life

- While someone explains a plan, your mind is already branching into alternatives.
- You link ideas from areas that seem unrelated.
- You spot potential in people before they see it themselves.
- Starting things is easier than finishing them.
- A completely settled situation can feel suffocating.
- Once a project is clearly working, your excitement fades and someone else can take over.

> The common factor in all these manifestations of intuition is a sort of ski jump: a soaring take-off from the known and established, ending in a swooping arrival at an advanced point, with the intervening steps apparently left out.
> — Isabel Myers, *Gifts Differing*, p. 57

## What it is not

**Not fickleness.** Myers argued that intuitives are as loyal to the inspiring possibility as sensing types are to the facts. Moving on is the point, not a flaw.

**Not introverted intuition.** Thomson calls them mirror images. Ne branches outward into many options and wants to share them in the first flush of discovery; Ni converges inward on one meaning and is least accessible while it is discovering.

**Not the casual meaning of "intuitive".** Thomson notes that many people called intuitive in everyday speech are actually observant sensing types or people with strong introverted feeling.

## When it leads

ENTPs and ENFPs lead with Ne. Thomson describes them as people who seem to fall in love when their imagination is engaged, and who are consistently surprised by the passionate responses they inspire. Jung pictured the extraverted intuitive as the natural advocate of every minority that holds the seed of future promise, kindling enthusiasm in others, "although he may have forsworn it by the morrow".

For INTPs and INFPs, Ne is the helper that carries their inner logic or values out into the world as possibilities.

## When it is the weakest note

For ISTJs and ISFJs, Ne is the inferior. Under strain it often appears as catastrophising: one worst-case scenario after another, or the fear that accepting one small change will make the whole structure collapse. Thomson describes this as inferior intuition persuading people that "the larger picture is at stake". Jung described the same function, repressed, as having "an amazing flair for every ambiguous, gloomy, dirty and dangerous possibility".

## In the shadow

Ne in a critical-parent role can terrify with forecasts of what will go wrong. Beebe uses the Wicked Witch of the West, forever threatening what she will do next. As an opposing personality it becomes contrarian "but what about…" used to block commitment rather than to explore.

## Growing it

If Ne is quiet for you, try writing down three options you would normally dismiss before any decision, without judging them yet. Myers' description of intuition used well is "seeing a possibility and bringing it to pass"; its misuse is dreaming up impossibilities that would provide an effortless solution.
